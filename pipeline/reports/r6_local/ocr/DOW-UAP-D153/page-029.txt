A beam splitter is a four-port device not only in the case of two incoming light modes 
interfering to produce two emerging light modes; a beam splitter is always a four-port 
device. Even if only one beam is split into two beams, if literally nothing behind the 
semitransparent mirror is interfering with the incident beam, quantum mechanically this 
nothing means a vacuum state. The very possibility that the second light mode behind 
the mirror might be excited makes a difference. The vacuum fluctuations carried by the 
empty mode (and entering the apparatus via the so-called unused input port of the 
beam splitter) do cause physical effects. Therefore, the vacuum fluctuations entering 
the second (unused) input port of the beam splitter must always be assigned a formal 
mode operator, a2, in order for the system to conserve energy, 2) obey the beam 1) 
splitter's aforementioned bosonic commutat ion relations and 3) guarantee that the two 
outgoing beams are independent bosonic light modes.
