3. Based on further analysis, the Photographic Division and this office do not
agree with the WADC evaluation of parhelions ("sun dogs") since the images do not
appear at the required angle of 22° or 46° nor are they in line horizontally with
the sum as would be required for the formetion of a parhelion (which would appear at
the intersection of the inner or outer halo circles - 22° and 46° respectively -
with the parhelic circle; the latter is in # horizontal plane coincidental with the
position of the sun). Additiomally, it will be noted in the photographs thet the
images display a flat portion which appears inverted from the corresponding portion
of the sun behing the cloud. Since a parhelion is the result of refraction through
ice crystals, the resulting image would not be inverted. Instead, it is our opinion

t the images are possibly a form of mirage caused by a hot air mass which

veled from left to right across the field of view and acted as a lens. However,
there is a small imge in the third photograph of the sequence which, if evaluated
as another image of the same type, would seemingly eliminate both parhelia and
mirages from consideration. This image is located directly above the seventh
telephone pole from the right at a vertical distance approximately equal to the
height of the pole. The minute size of this image militates strongly against the
mirage theory since the hot air mass would have had to turn about 90° and proceed
away from the camera in order to produce this effect. Therefore, further analysis
appears to be justified. Suggest the weer a enienen yj be asked for an opinion.
