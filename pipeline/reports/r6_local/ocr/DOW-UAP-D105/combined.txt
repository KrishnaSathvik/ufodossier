General Background: Driving from Washington, D.C. to Portland, Oregon, on

the luggage compartment of the car and got my camera out of a suit-
case. Loading it hurriedly, I exposed approximately thirty feet of
film. There was no reference point in the sky and it was impossible
for me to make any estimate of speed, size, altitude or distance.
Toward the end one of the objects reversed course and proceeded away
from the group. I held the camera still and allowed this single

one to cross the field of view, picking it up again and repeating
for three or four such passes. By this time all of the objects

had disappeared. I expended the balance of the film late that
afternoon on a mountain somewhere in Idaho.

Dear Sir:

In connection with "flying saucer" newspaper accounts of
observations on the search radar system at the Washington
Netional Airport, it occurred to me that the described be-
havior could possibly be accounted for in the following manner:

High power, narrow beam fire control radar systems of the usual
type might conceivably, under unusual atmospheric conditions,
create enough ionization along points on the beam in the upper
atmosphere to allow the return of an appreciabdle echo to the
search radar system from such points. The apparent motion would
be quite haphazard, following whatever the fire control radar
operator does in directing the beam.

This hypothesis is unattractive for several reasons: Pirstly,
the power required for such an effect is many tines greater
than that put out by the usual radar, Secondly, such echoes
would presumably come from ionospheric heights and therefore
could not appear at ranges of a few miles from the search radar,
aS was reported. Thirdly, it seems unlikely that a coincident
visual effect of any conspicuous magnitude would be produced,

In spite of this, I am offering thie suggestion in the belief
that if considerable time, money and effort is being devoted to
this problem, it might be worthwhile trying the relatively simple
experiment of having a powerful fire control radar sweep out
certain simple patterns to see if they oan be detected on the
search radar system, preferably at a time when “flying saucer*
echoes are being observed.

Very truly yours,

s/

I should like to present te you what I believe to
be a tenable theory as to the origin and nature of the

radiation of sufficient power density is capable of
ionizing gas at low pressure. Upon deionization, the
es emits radiation of characteristic wave lengths, some
of which lie in the visible spectrum. Further, it is
known that radar equipment can detect ionized gas, such as
northern lights, or charged water droplets, such as appear
in thunderclouds.

I dDelieve that ionization of dise-shaped segments of
air in the upper atmosphere may be caused by beams from a r
radar transmitter held in a fixed position or slowly traversed, i
instead of the usual sweeping motion.

To verify the possibility, I propose in the very near
future to conduct an experiment. A glass sphere containing
air at atmosphere pressure will be exposed to radiation of a
known wave length and power density. The pressure in the
sphere will then be reduced until ionization occurs. The
screen of a separate radar transmitter-receiver will also
be observed to note whether the change in the condition of
the air is detectable by radar.

Very truly yours,
