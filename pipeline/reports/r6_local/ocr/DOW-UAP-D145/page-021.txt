CLASSIC NUCLEAR FUSION SCHEMES 
Nuclear fusion, which powers the Sun and the stars, begins with the collision of two 
lightwe ight atomic nuclei to create two new part icles with the release of energy. As an 
example, if two specific isotopes of hydrogen (tritium and deuterium) were to collide, 
the reaction would produce a neutron plus an alpha particle (ionized helium nucleus).

The 17.6 MeV of energy is split between the kinetic energy of the neutron (14.1 MeV) 
and the helium nucleus (3.5 MeV) based on conservat ion of energy and conservation of 
momentum. The kinetic energy is eventually converted into heat in a fusion reactor. 
The 14.1 -MeV neutron will penetrate far into lead or steel shielding and can cause 
considerable material damage. The ionized helium nucleus, however, will not go very 
far through any material without being absorbed and dissipating its energy as heat.
