Payload Cost Estimate for Lightcraft Launch Using a 10 MW CO2/Gas 
Mixture Laser 
Froning and Davis [26] used a proposed 10 MW electron gun-driven N2/CO2/H 2 laser 
design to estimate the Lightcraft payload launch cost per kilogram, which is described 
in what follows. Each power oscillator optics module transmitting a 2.5 MW beam of 
10.6 µm wavelength photons generates 1.334 x 1026 photons/sec, and 15 kg/sec of CO2 
mass flow represents 2.053 x 1026 molecules/sec of gas flow. These figures taken 
together mean that 1.54 CO2 molecules are required to lase one photon. A 2.5 MW 
laser operating for 300 seconds of thrusting will allow us to send 5.25 kg of payload 
into LEO, and the total laser energy (E1aser) output is 750 MJ. If the laser efficiency is 
0.20, then we will need to use 12.5 MW of electrical power for 300 seconds (or 3,750 
MJ of total energy), which, at a cost of $0.10 per kWh (or $0.0278 per MJ), gives a 
total cost of $104 for the required electrical energy to launch the payload.
