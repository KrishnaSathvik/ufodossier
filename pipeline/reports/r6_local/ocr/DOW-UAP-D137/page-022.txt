In 1973, a new chemical laser technology, based on deuterium fluoride (DF), was 
determined to be scalable to high power at mid- infrared wavelengths which propagate 
far better in the atmosphere than CO2 laser beams. The Navy and ARPA jointly built a 
multi-hundred-kW class laser and mated it with a 70 cm beam director. In March 1978, 
this integrated system succeeded destroying operational TOW anti-tank missiles in 
flight. 
The Navy then used this technology to produce the nation's first MW-class HEL weapon 
test bed. The Mid-lnfraRed Advanced Chemical Laser (MIRACL, Figure 15) and the 
Sealite Beam Director (SLBD, Figure 11) were installed and integrated at White Sands 
Missile Range in the mid-1980s and used for experiments by the Department of 
Defense. Subsonic drones and multi-mach missiles as well as strapped down ICBM 
boosters were among the many targets successfully engaged.
