I am inclosing two copies of the report you asked for, and hope

We have made a preliminary examination of the eighteen collections
taken at nine locations on my. Monday-Tuesday trip. There was a larce
number of copper=bearing particles on one collection (R-104L) talren on
Highway 84, seventeen miles north of Highway 66, and scattered occase
ional particles throughout the series. The collection taken at the
same location immediately following Rel104L shows practically no copper.

A large part of the R-104L is of a granular material unfamiliar to us, and
the copper indications originate principally in what are apparently
agerecationsa of finely (one-micron) granular material of overall sizes

in the neighborhood of thirty microns. Some copper partizles, howe

ever, appear to be solid and opaque. The passage of two automobiles while
this collection was being made is a complicating factor. It is hard

to see how they micht have been a source of copper, however, unless it

was a question of stirring up some from dust on the pavement,

We intend to do some further chemical work with these collections,
but I doubt if much new relevant information will be obtained.

My tentative conclusion is that no widespread significant dispersion
of copper@=bearing particles was present in the atmosphere in the rezion
covered at the time the collections were made. No definite statement
is possible about the one heavy copper collection: it should be presumed
to be of local origin until some evidence to the contrary is found.

The route of the collecting trip included Pastura, Santa Rosa, junc-
tion of Routes 66 and 84, end Las Vegas on Monday afternoon. On Tuesday
morning it ineluded Las Vegas, Canoncito, Glorieta and Pecos.

COBALT AND

In the 2:00 pem, collection of July 26, three quite remarkable particles were
found that gave very strong cobalt indieations. The particles were apparently per-
fect spheres (at least they were perfectly circular in cross section), twelve mic-
rons in diameter. The reaction with rubeanic acid, after ammonie treatment, pro-
duced a dense yellow-tan halo, sixty microns in diameter. The color was not quite
so clear es with pure cobalt: the appearance suggesting a hich=cobalt composition
with some metal present, Such particles are quite unique in the experience of the
presents, If these particles were of meteoritic origin they cml1d, in the absence
of vertical air movement, bave fallen a distance of the order of 20,000 feet in an
interval of 41.5 hours.

Two particles giving nickel indications were found, This is quite normal, hor-
ever. If anything, the number is smaller than miczht have been expected in material
of surface origin. The collections wore not given acid treatment: additional nickel
indications might have been found if this had been done,
